Store revenue since June 2022 is ₹5.05 crore across 21,118 orders. Retention channels means email and WhatsApp through BiteSpeed. Returning customers means any order from someone who had bought before, whatever prompted it.

The 10% figure is not correct

Retention is reported inside Boond as 10% of revenue. Measured on the last quarter it is 2.4%. Even if the entire lifetime earnings of every automation, ₹5,01,739 built up over twelve months, were credited to this one quarter alone, the answer would be 8.7%. There is no way to reach 10%. The number that is close to 10% is a different one: 25.0% of revenue comes from returning customers, and almost none of that is being caused by email or WhatsApp.

What is already working

These two halves depend on each other. Campaigns create the visit and flows convert it, so a campaign sent while the automations are off wastes most of the traffic it creates. Every automation needs to be revived before the sending calendar restarts, and the delivery failure has to be fixed first, because it is silently halving every campaign.

Why the live popup fails

The offer running today

The popup asks a first-time visitor to buy four or more perfumes to earn 10% off. That is a basket of roughly ₹6,000 against an average order of ₹2,563. The offer is out of reach, and it is asked for the moment the page loads, before the visitor has seen a product.

The version that captured 3.86% offered a flat ₹200 off with no minimum.

Part 1 · Acquisition

1

Grow the list, then greet it properly

Fix the popup offer, and add Alia if the fix alone does not lift capture. Then a welcome flow with real messaging, on WhatsApp first because almost everyone is reachable there, and on email once sending has restarted.

Part 2 · Recovery

2

Catch the traffic that is already arriving

Browse, collection, abandoned cart and abandoned checkout all running, so the most is recovered from the visitors Boond is already paying for. Fix the WhatsApp delivery failure at the same time, because half of every message is currently lost.

Part 3 · Activation

3

Stay with the customer after the order

Keep them engaged once they have bought, show them how to wear and layer the attars properly, and bring them back sooner. This is what pulls the median time between orders down from 141 days.

Part 4 · Replenishment and cross-sell

4

Never let a customer pass their churn window

Reach them before they drift. Replenish what they finished, cross-sell the fragrance they have not tried, and use new launches to keep the reason to return fresh.

Part 5 · Win-back

5

Go and get the customers who stopped

13,128 customers have not ordered in six months. Offers and the right message bring a share of them back, and that is what lifts the returning customer rate directly.
